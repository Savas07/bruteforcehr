Context: the documents `@bfhr/cv-render` prints for every surface (desktop and iOS preview, PDF export, DOCX, M4 email attachments). User goal: "send a CV that looks professional and that an ATS reads correctly". Readers: recruiters skimming for 6–10 s, and ATS parsers. Stage: refinement → final. Protocol: lighter M2 protocol (iterate, then the full matrix once); no Mobbin round, the template research is in `docs/design/research/cv-editor.md`.

Captures judged (`pnpm --filter @bfhr/cv-render critique`, gitignored):

## Design Critique: CV templates

### Overall Impression

All three read as calm, conventional CVs: name first, standard headings, dates aligned right, one column. Classic is the safe default, modern feels a little warmer through the accent name, compact fits the EN CV on one A4 page. The biggest problems were not visual but print-engine parity: WebKit printed everything 6.7 % larger (other page breaks) and broke the Turkish name in the text layer.

### Usability (does the document do its job: skim + parse)

### Visual Hierarchy

- **What draws the eye first**: the name (serif 22–24 pt; accent colour in modern). Correct.
- **Reading flow**: name → role → contact → summary → sections; within entries role (bold) → dates (right, muted, tabular figures) → company · location (muted) → bullets. Matches how recruiters scan.
- **Emphasis**: headings are distinct but quiet (classic: accent capitals with rule; modern: larger accent serif, no rule; compact: small capitals with hairline). Placeholders are the only tinted element, which is right: they must be filled before sending.

### Consistency

| Element  | Issue                                                                                                                                                                         | Recommendation                                  |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Dates    | One format per language in all templates and in DOCX ("Mar 2022 – Present", "März 2022 – heute", "Mar 2022 – Günümüz"), engine-independent tables.                            | None.                                           |
| Headings | Same dictionary as the desktop ATS check (`SECTION_HEADINGS`).                                                                                                                | None.                                           |
| Spacing  | All gaps derive from one density scale; margins from `pageGeometry` used by `@page`, DOCX and native paginators.                                                              | None.                                           |
| DOCX     | Same order and words as HTML; Word styles instead of CSS. Quick Look substitutes Times for Calibri/Cambria and ignores the right tab stop, so dates sit next to titles there. | Owner check in Microsoft Word (see Resolution). |

### Accessibility

- **Color contrast**: body ink #1B1F24 16.6:1; muted #4B5261 7.8:1; every curated accent ≥ 6.4:1 (navy 6.9, teal 7.0, green 6.4, blue 6.7, plum 9.4, burgundy 8.6, brown 7.4, graphite 12.7); custom accents are darkened to ≥ 4.5:1; placeholder text 14.6:1 on #FFF1B8 with a 4.7:1 outline and the literal brackets (not colour alone). Rules under headings are decorative.
- **Touch targets**: n/a (static document; links are text).
- **Text readability**: body 9.3–10.5 pt, smallest text (compact contact line) 8.6 pt, line height 1.3–1.45, no hyphenation, no italics, no justified text.
- **Structure**: `lang` = CV language, `h1` name → `h2` sections → `h3` entries, lists are `ul/li`, photo `alt=""` beside the name heading, Chromium PDFs are tagged.

### What Works Well

- Standard, single-column structure that reads back 100 % in both engines and all three languages.
- Turkish and German typography handled in the text itself (İ/I, ß→SS, umlauts), so the PDF text layer, DOCX and the ATS check agree.
- Placeholders are impossible to miss but don't scream.

### Priority Recommendations

1. **Fix the WebKit text layer for Turkish** — a misread name is the worst ATS failure.
2. **Match WebKit print scale to Chromium** — preview, macOS/iOS and server PDFs must paginate the same.
3. **Keep headings with their first item in every engine** — stranded headings look careless.

## Resolution

All 🔴 and 🟡 findings fixed in `packages/cv-render/src/templates.ts` / `html.ts` / `docx.ts` (commits on `m2/cv-render`); 🟢 compact photo fixed, the other two 🟢 logged as intended behaviour.

Verification after the fixes (`artifacts/cv-render/report.txt`, 2026-10-23): 24/24 cases read back every heading and entry in order in Chromium **and** WebKit, page counts identical per case (13 × 1 page, 11 × 2 pages), first-line font sizes and positions within 0.5 pt between engines, no broken glyphs. CI keeps the Chromium half (`src/ats.test.ts`); the WebKit half runs in `critique` on macOS and in the app exporters' own tests.

Exit criteria: zero Critical, zero open Moderate. **Met.**

Owner: open one exported DOCX (e.g. `artifacts/cv-render/a4/classic-de-full.docx`) in Microsoft Word once to confirm Calibri/Cambria and the right-aligned dates; Quick Look can't show either.
