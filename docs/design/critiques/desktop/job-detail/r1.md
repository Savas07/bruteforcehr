# Job detail (desktop) — critique round 1

- **Screen:** Jobs list + Job detail split view (`/jobs`, `/jobs/$jobId`), detail pane is the core screen.
- **User goal:** decide quickly whether a job is worth applying to, and act (apply, save, not for me).
- **Persona:** active job seeker, often stressed and short on time, switching between phone and desktop.
- **Stage:** refinement. **Focus:** the match explanation, the action row and the 13" (1280 × 800) layout.

## Design Critique: Jobs — job detail

### Overall Impression
The pane reads as calm and trustworthy: band label first, "88 of 100" as a quiet caption, the segmented bar, evidence quotes and "Found on" with the direct-from-employer badge. The biggest problem is the 13" window: the pane is under 760 px, the Details card folds above the match and pushes the core of the screen (the match) below the fold, and several buttons wrap their ↗ / chevron under the label.

### Usability
| Finding | Severity | Recommendation |
|---------|----------|----------------|
| At 1280 × 800 the folded Details card (7 facts in a 2-column card with its own heading) fills the first screen; "How this job fits" starts at y ≈ 620 and its content is below the fold. The persona decides from the match, not from relocation status. | 🔴 Critical | Fold the facts into a borderless 3-column strip without a visible heading, and drop facts the header already says (salary, work mode, where, employment type). ### Visual Hierarchy
- **What draws the eye first:** the filled teal "Apply on company site" and the selected list row. Correct for a decision screen; at 1280 the match should be next but the Details card wins (fixed above).
- **Reading flow:** company · posted → title → chips → salary → actions → (facts) → match → description → sources. On wide panes the rail (Details, second Apply, Found on) sits beside the match, which is the intended Contra layout.
- **Emphasis:** "Strong match" at title2 with "88 of 100" in secondary callout keeps the number secondary. The band bar's current segment is the only teal block. Good.

### Consistency
| Element | Issue | Recommendation |
|---------|-------|----------------|
| Tags | Impact tags are bordered chips while source badges are filled `SourceTag`s; two tag styles side by side in one pane. | Impact as inline text (above); keep `SourceTag` for sources. |
| Buttons | Trailing icons (↗, ⌄) don't match `Button`'s leading-icon layout. | Inline icons, same size (16 px) and stroke 1.75. |
| Copy | "Hidden" in the caught-up copy vs sidebar "Hidden jobs" (D10). | Use "Hidden jobs" everywhere. |

### Accessibility
- **Color contrast:** all text uses allowed pairings (fg/secondary/tertiary on base, accent text on accent subtle, warning text on base). The disabled Apply in dark mode is exempt but unreadable, so the reason must be in text (fixed).
- **Touch targets:** row actions are 24 px icon buttons (desktop minimum), action row 32 px.
- **Text readability:** body 14/20, footnotes 12/16; evidence quotes in tertiary 12 px are the smallest meaningful text and pass AA.

### What Works Well
- Band first, "88 of 100" second, no "%", no gauge; the segmented bar labels every segment so colour isn't the only cue.
- "Based on CV "Base CV — English" · updated 7 hours ago · Change" makes the score's basis and freshness visible.
- Evidence under every requirement and factor ("From the posting: …", "From your CV: …") makes the AI checkable; the AI block is labelled "AI-assisted · AI summary · can be wrong" with 👍/👎.
- "Found on 2 sites" groups Direct from employer above Job boards, tags "Used for Apply", and the footnote explains differences.
- Not for me is instant: the row collapses to "Hidden: … · Undo · Why?", the next job opens, and the optional reason popover has Skip and Save reason at equal weight with number keys.

### Priority Recommendations
1. **Get the match above the fold at 1280** — compact facts strip, narrower default list.
2. **Fix wrapped icons in buttons** — the primary action must read as one control.
3. **Say why Apply is disabled offline in place** — the persona shouldn't have to hover to learn it.
