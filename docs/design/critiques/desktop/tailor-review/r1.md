## Design Critique: Tailor review

### Overall Impression

The WRITER/Grammarly layout works at 1280 × 800: the CV renders at readable size on the left with the accepted edit already in place, and the 360 px rail lists one card per edit with its location ("Experience · Pazarbox · bullet 2"), the struck original, the suggestion, the Why line and what it is based on. Decided cards collapse to "Accepted · Decide again" / "Original kept", so the eye goes to what is left.

### Usability

| 1. The Jobs search, filters and scopes stayed above the review and took ~150 px of a "full content area" view.                                                        | 🟡 Moderate | **Fixed:** the feed header steps aside while tailoring (`job-detail/Screen.tsx`).                                                                                                |

### Visual Hierarchy

- **First:** "Tailor your CV for Fernweh Reisen", then the preview with the change in place, then the open card.
- **Emphasis:** one primary ("Save as new version"); Accept is secondary, Keep original a text button; the AI badge and the disclosure sit in the rail header.

### Consistency

| Element | Issue                                                                                                           | Recommendation |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| Preview | Same `PreviewPane` as CV Studio (zoom, page breaks).                                                            | —              |
| Diff    | Struck original + labelled "Original"/"Suggestion" text, never colour alone, as in CV Studio's tracked changes. | —              |

### Accessibility

- axe: 0 critical · 0 serious · 0 moderate · 0 minor in all 20 runs; "needs review" items are the shell's keycap and the preview's shadow-root text.
- Cards are focusable articles labelled by their location; J/K move, A/R decide, ⌘↵ saves, Esc closes.

### What Works Well

- Grounding is visible ("Based on: Summary"), skipped edits explain themselves calmly, and the CV itself never changes: the version is pinned on the application.
